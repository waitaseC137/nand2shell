# 👾 CWE-1384 — Improper Handling of Physical or Environmental Conditions

> **The physical world did not keep to the contract.** A circuit is designed
> assuming a certain range of temperature, voltage and environment. When that
> range is left — whether naturally or because someone left it on purpose — the
> circuit produces a wrong result.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Official name** | Improper Handling of Physical or Environmental Conditions |
| **Abstraction level** | *Class* |
| **Parent** | CWE-703 — Improper Check or Handling of Exceptional Conditions |
| **Its children in the catalogue** | [1247](./cwe_1247.md) · [1261](./cwe_1261.md) |

---

## What NandGame Assumes

Every gate you built at the start of the series is assumed to be **perfect**: it
works instantly, uses no energy, makes no mistakes. Without that assumption
nothing could be taught — but it is an assumption.

1384 is exactly **the name of the place where that assumption breaks.**

A real gate depends on these:

```
supply voltage   ·   clock signal   ·   temperature
electromagnetic environment   ·   radiation   ·   the age of the part
```

If one of these leaves the design range, the gate still "works" — it just works
wrongly. And the circuit does not notice, because it has no organ to notice
with.

> 🔑 This is not a software bug. The code can be flawless, the logic can be
> flawless. What breaks is **the ground the code stands on.**
> [The abstraction ladder in 03.5](../salterden_bilgisayara/03.5_soyutlama_merdiveni.md)
> collapses from below here.

---

## Deliberate or Natural — the Circuit Cannot Tell

The two children in your catalogue sit under the same umbrella but come from very
different places:

| | [CWE-1247](./cwe_1247.md) | [CWE-1261](./cwe_1261.md) |
|---|---|---|
| **What happens** | The voltage/clock breaks for an instant | A charged particle flips a cell |
| **Who does it** | **An attacker** — takes the device in hand and does it | **The universe** — cosmic rays, radiation |
| **When** | Whenever they want, on target | At random, unpredictably |
| **Lesson** | [02 · All the Gates from One Brick](../salterden_bilgisayara/02_nanddan_kapilar.md) | [04 · When Wires Become Numbers](../salterden_bilgisayara/04_teller_sayi_olunca.md) |

One is an attack, the other an accident. But **from the circuit's point of view
both are the same event:** a bit came out different from what was expected.

> 🔑 The practical consequence of this split is: **the same defence closes both.**
> The error-correcting code put in place against particle strikes also catches
> the glitch an attacker produces. That is exactly the benefit of looking at the
> umbrella — you only notice that two separate threats can be met with a single
> mechanism once you see that they are related.

## The Other Two Children

The two that MITRE lists under this umbrella and that are not yet in your
catalogue:

- **CWE-1332 — Improper Handling of Faults that Lead to Instruction Skips.** The
  glitch is so precise that the processor **skips** an instruction. If the skipped
  instruction is a check — say the "is the password correct" branch — the check
  is never performed. A neighbour of [1247](./cwe_1247.md), a more precise form
  of the same technique.
- **CWE-1351 — Improper Handling of Hardware Behavior in Exceptionally Cold
  Environments.** In extreme cold, memory holds its data for longer. Freeze the
  device and switch it off, and the contents of RAM stay readable not for seconds
  but for minutes.

Both are variations of the same sentence: **the design assumes an envelope, the
attacker steps outside the envelope.**

---

## Why It Has Its Own Page

This is a class, so you do not label a real vulnerability with 1384 — by
[the hierarchy rule](./README.md#the-hierarchy-between-cwes) the most concrete
tier is chosen.

But it has **two children at once** in your catalogue, and at first glance the
two look unrelated: one is an attack technique, the other a particle from space.
Without 1384 the link between them would be invisible.

> 💡 [CWE-682](./cwe_682.md) was written for the same reason: overflow, underflow
> and off-by-one look like three separate things at first glance, yet all of them
> belong to the "the calculation is wrong" family.

---

## How to Prevent It

At the umbrella level, the defence comes down to the pair **"protect the physical
assumption, or notice that it has broken"**:

- **Protect the envelope.** Voltage regulation, clock filtering, shielding,
  temperature control. Make it physically harder for the attacker or the
  environment to break the range.
- **Detect that it has broken.** Voltage and clock sensors, temperature
  thresholds. The circuit monitors its own operating conditions and stops when it
  leaves the range.
- **Verify the result.** Computing a critical result twice and comparing,
  using error-correcting code (ECC), keeping a parity bit. A single flipped bit is
  caught this way — whatever its source.
- **Do not rely on a single check.** Skipping one instruction should not bring
  down all of security; testing a critical decision in two different places makes
  glitch attacks expensive.

> ⚠️ None of these is a software patch. The defences here are **hardware and
> design** decisions; they cannot be closed later with software.

## Summary — Keep in Mind

```
☐ CWE-1384 is a CLASS: "a physical/environmental condition left the design range".
☐ NandGame assumes perfect gates; 1384 is the name of the place where that assumption breaks.
☐ The code can be flawless — what breaks is the GROUND the code stands on.
☐ The two children in your catalogue: 1247 (voltage/clock glitch) · 1261 (particle strike).
☐ An ATTACKER does one, the UNIVERSE does the other — but the circuit cannot tell them apart.
☐ That is why the same defence closes both (e.g. ECC catches both the particle and the glitch).
☐ Its other children: 1332 (instruction skip) · 1351 (memory retention in extreme cold).
☐ The shared sentence: the design assumes an ENVELOPE, the attacker steps outside it.
☐ The defence has three sides: protect the envelope · detect that it broke · verify the result.
☐ These are hardware/design decisions; they cannot be closed with a software patch.
```
