# 👾 CWE-697 — Incorrect Comparison

> **The comparison is wrong.** The weakness on this page is not a single bug but
> a **pillar**: two things are compared and the answer does not reflect reality.
> It is not enough for the calculation to be right — the way the result of the
> calculation is **read** can be wrong too.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Official name** | Incorrect Comparison |
| **Abstraction level** | **Pillar** — MITRE's top tier |
| **Parent** | None. Pillars are the top of the hierarchy. |
| **Its children in the catalogue** | [1023](./cwe_1023.md) · [1254](./cwe_1254.md) |
| **Where it is born** | [15 · Condition](../salterden_bilgisayara/15_condition.md) — which of the three outcomes count |

## 682's Sibling

Your catalogue has two pillars, and both were born in the same unit. NandGame's
ALU does two jobs, and both have a counterpart in MITRE's top tier:

```
What does the ALU do?
   │
   ├─ CALCULATES       →  CWE-682  Incorrect Calculation
   │                        addition, subtraction, increment
   │
   └─ COMPARES         →  CWE-697  Incorrect Comparison
                            less, equal, greater
```

The idea of a pillar itself was explained on
[682's page](./cwe_682.md#what-a-pillar-means) — the four-tier tree, and that a
pillar's job is not to label bugs but **to show kinship**. It will not be
repeated here.

This page asks a different question: **why is comparison a separate pillar?**

---

## A Comparison Is Not a Calculation

At first glance it looks as if 697 ought to sit under 682. After all, a
comparison is a subtraction — comparing `a` with `b` means computing `a − b`.

But MITRE made them separate pillars, and it is right. Here is why:

> **A comparison is two steps: compute, then read. The bug can be in either one —
> and the two are independent of each other.**

```
step 1:  X = a − b             the calculation can be right   ← if it breaks here: 682
step 2:  look at X and decide  but the reading can be wrong   ← if it breaks here: 697
```

In [lesson 15](../salterden_bilgisayara/15_condition.md) you built this
distinction as a circuit. The ALU did the subtraction — that part was flawless.
The Condition circuit read the result. And the whole question was whether the
**reading** was right.

> 🔑 Reading a correctly computed number wrongly is as dangerous as reading a
> wrongly computed number correctly. Both lead to the same place: a wrong
> decision.

### What the circuit showed

The most instructive thing about the Condition level is that comparison is **not
a single operation**. There were three independent detections — `X < 0`,
`X = 0`, `X > 0` — and three separate permissions decided which of them would
count.

Every permission is a breaking point. Forget to open `gt` and your `X ≥ 0` test
silently turns into an `X = 0` test. The calculation is still right, the circuit
still works, the answer is wrong.

---

## Three Breaking Points

697 has nine children, and they all fall under three questions. When reviewing,
ask these three in order:

### ① What did you compare?

Is the thing being compared the thing that should be compared?

| CWE | what happens |
|---|---|
| **1025** Comparison Using Wrong Factors | The right object, but the **wrong property** is compared |
| **1024** Comparison of Incompatible Types | Two types that cannot be converted into each other are compared |
| **581** Just One of Equals and Hashcode | Two objects are "equal" but their hashes differ |

The classic example: comparing two strings not by their content but by **their
address in memory**. Two separate copies carrying the same text come out
"different".

### ② Did you compare enough?

Were all the factors that would change the answer looked at?

| CWE | what happens |
|---|---|
| [**1023**](./cwe_1023.md) Incomplete Comparison with Missing Factors | A factor was not asked about ✓ *you have it* |
| **183** Permissive List of Allowed Inputs | The allow list is wider than it should be |
| **185** Incorrect Regular Expression | The pattern does not fully describe what it should match |

The sneaky part of this group: **there is a check.** In an audit it gets ticked
off as "a comparison is made here" and passed. What is missing is not the check
itself but its scope.

The **OF debt** closed in lesson 15 falls exactly here: looking at the sign bit
and not at the overflow flag is an incomplete comparison. The answer is usually
right — it is only wrong on the day it overflows.

### ③ How did you compare?

What does the **manner** of the comparison leak, what does it miss?

| CWE | what happens |
|---|---|
| [**1254**](./cwe_1254.md) Incorrect Comparison Logic Granularity | Compared piece by piece, the time taken leaks information ✓ *you have it* |
| **1077** Floating Point Comparison with Incorrect Operator | Floating-point numbers are compared with `==` |
| **1039** Adversarial Input Perturbations | A recognition mechanism cannot tell deliberately perturbed input apart |

The third group is the least expected, because here **the answer is right**. The
problem is not in the answer but in the way it is reached.

---

## Why It Has Its Own Page

You do not label a real vulnerability with 697; the most concrete tier is chosen
([the hierarchy rule](./README.md#the-hierarchy-between-cwes)). If a factor is
missing, [1023](./cwe_1023.md); if it is granularity, [1254](./cwe_1254.md).

There are two reasons it has a page:

**① Half of the tree.** The backbone of the catalogue rests on two pillars: 682
calculates, 697 compares. NandGame's ALU unit builds exactly these two. Had 682
been written and 697 not, half of the map would have had no name.

**② The three questions themselves.** The *"what · enough · how"* split above is
not written in any of the children. Each looks through its own narrow window.
Seeing all three together changes the questions you ask while reading code.

---

## How to Prevent It

- **Read a comparison as two steps.** First "what was computed", then "how was
  the result read". Verify the two separately; one being right does not make the
  other right.
- **Count all the factors.** List everything that goes into the decision, then see
  that each one is asked about in the code. What is missing is what is on the
  list but not in the code.
- **Test with boundary values.** Comparison bugs do not show in the middle; they
  show at the point of equality, at the point of overflow, on empty input.
- **If you compare secret data, fix the time.** Passwords, session keys, signature
  checks — "exit early" is forbidden for these ([1254](./cwe_1254.md)).
- **Align the types before comparing.** Signed/unsigned, narrow/wide, text/number:
  do the conversion **before** the comparison, and explicitly
  ([CWE-681](./cwe_681.md)).
- **Put the code that defines equality in one place.** If the same "are they
  equal" question is repeated in three places, it has to be right in all three.
